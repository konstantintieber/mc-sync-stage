## Abstract

A German city administration wants to enable its own citizen developers to automate internal and citizen-facing processes, using self-hosted n8n Community, fully air-gapped, with no cloud models, no external APIs, and Article 9 case data involved.

We hold no credentials to their environment: no VPN, no read or write access, only dummy data censored by their own staff before it reaches us. Specialist government software (Fachverfahren) is either sealed off or priced out of reach, so most of the automation value sits in the manual work around those systems, not inside them.

This session walks through the enablement model we're using to work within those constraints: a week onsite teaching n8n fundamentals to non-developers, then remote debugging support, with onsite return visits when remote isn't enough. You'll hear what's actually teachable in a week, and why the human and process work mattered more than the technical build.n.

## Takeaway

Understand the escalation model for enabling automation in fully restricted environments: teach onsite, support remotely, return to co-build only when necessary

Discover what's actually teachable in one week to people with no development background, and which concepts consistently need a second pass

Learn the failure modes citizen developers hit most often once they're building on their own

Identify why end-to-end process thinking and stakeholder alignment matter more than the technical build itself in heterogeneous, multi-party environments
