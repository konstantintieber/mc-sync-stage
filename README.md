# Sync stage MCs

Useful resources for the sync stage moderators (MC) Aya and Konsti.

What an MC does for speakers:
- **Greets you backstage**, about 10 minutes before your session. They check how to say your name, your title and their intro line. *They are also there to calm the nerves.*
- **Introduces you** in about 30 seconds: your name, company, the title of your talk and one reason to listen.
- **Watches the clock** during your session. *When your time is up, they walk on, so the next speaker gets their full slot.*
- **Closes your session**: thanks you and tells the room where to go next.
- **Asks if you're heading to the Community Lounge afterwards** (the Solution Zone for the Builders Stage). *If you say yes, they announce it, so people with questions know where to find you.*

## Cue card for MCs

**Front: your stage, your pair.** One of you hosts, one of you is side-stage. Swap per session however you like. Speakers own their slot, you own everything around it, including getting people to the right place at the right time.

**Before the day**

- Read the abstracts for your stage. You can introduce every speaker in two sentences without notes.
- Your split is on the Assignment per stage page. Done.
- 12 October (invite sent): 16:00 check in and pick up your T-shirt at registration. 16:30 your stage, 30 minutes with the stage manager and tech crew: the mics, the clicker, the speaker screen and the exits. 17:00 team walkthrough. 18:00 speaker stage tour from registration: show your speakers the stage and the speaker lounge. Lounge rules: one lounge for two stages, go in only when called, stay quiet because only a curtain separates it from the stage.

**On the day**

- 08:15: morning shift at your stage. The afternoon shift arrives before the lunch handover. Test your handheld mic and the clicker. Check that the speaker screen shows the slides and the timer.
- T-10: C2's wrangler brings the speaker backstage to the stage manager, who mics them up. You greet them and calm the nerves: how to say their name, their title, your intro line. Quietly ask if they'll head to the Community Lounge after the talk (on Builders: the Solution Zone). The stage manager counts you in.
- T-2: call the room. "We start in two minutes. Phones to silent, come closer."
- Open: 30 seconds. Name, company, title of the talk, one reason to listen. Hand over to applause.
- During: the host watches the clock. Side-stage watches the room and the stage WhatsApp group.
- Close: thank the speaker. No Q&A, every talk fills its slot. If the speaker said yes: "[Name] is heading to the Community Lounge now, catch them there."
- Send: say what's next on this stage, what's on elsewhere, and where to go now (lunch, the next session, the activations).
- Between talks: say goodbye, leave the stage, come back 3 to 5 minutes before the next one. Pick the activation to suit the room. Anything that makes people move works.
- Overtime: at 0 you walk on. "That's our time, and I want to make sure the next speaker gets theirs. [Name] will be in the Community Lounge for questions." Smile, mean it, don't negotiate.

**Your merch bag**: about 15 caps and beanies per stage, for the best trivia answers and as filler when tech fails. Throw underarm, towards the aisle.

**When things go wrong**

- Speaker missing at T-10: not your job. C2's wrangler finds them and escalates, and you'll see it in your stage's WhatsApp group. Don't leave your stage to look.
- Tech fails: you talk, the tech crew fixes. Use the trivia on the back, or throw some merch. Two minutes of trivia beats two minutes of silence.
- Running late overall: protect the next speaker, not the current one.
- Room full: fill the front rows first, then send people to the next session elsewhere.

**Back, stage lines:** Builders: lightning slots are 15 minutes including changeover, so 12 minutes of talk and 3 to swap. Run the block from the side of the stage with the next speaker already mic'd, and send the room to lunch at 12:00. Automation: partner speakers get the same clock as everyone; your intro sets the tone: what they built, what broke, what they'd do differently.

### The back, on both cards

Four ways to activate your crowd. They work for you, and for any speaker who asks how to get the room going.

1. **Start with a hand.** One "raise your hand if" question in the first 30 seconds. It tells you who's in the room, and it tells them they're allowed to react.
2. **Make them turn.** One 60-second neighbour prompt with a specific question: "Tell the person next to you about the last workflow you broke." (On Partner Day: "…about the last client project that almost broke.") Vague prompts ("introduce yourself") fall flat.
3. **Name the room.** Call back something from earlier today: a line from the keynote, the talk before this one, the coffee queue. It proves you're here, not on autopilot.
4. **End with an ask.** One concrete thing to do in the next ten minutes: find the speaker afterwards, try the one node they showed, or open the app.
